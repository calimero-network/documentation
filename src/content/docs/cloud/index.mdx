Calimero Cloud runs the clusters your apps run on, and the console at
[cloud.calimero.network](https://cloud.calimero.network/signin) is where you publish packages, deploy them, create
instances and invite your team.

## Shared and dedicated clusters

| | Shared cluster | Dedicated cluster |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who runs on it** | Many developers' apps side by side, each account within its quotas | One customer |
| **Billing** | Per use, with a free allowance every month | A flat monthly fee |
| **Nodes** | Calimero's attested TDX nodes, replicas spread across zones | Your own attested TDX nodes |

Every cluster is self-contained: its own accounts, passkeys, organizations and storage key. An organization and its
instances live on one cluster, and every shared app of a Cloud organization runs on the same shared cluster. See
[Calimero Cloud pricing](https://calimero.network/cloud/#pricing).

## Two kinds of sign-in

- **The console** signs you in with your work account. It knows your Cloud organization, its members, its packages
  and its billing.
- **The cluster** signs you in with a **passkey**. An app instance belongs to your account on the cluster, and only
  your passkey can create one, approve an upgrade or mint an invite. So the console prepares those steps and your
  browser runs them against the cluster: Calimero, which operates the cluster, holds no credential to your apps.

## Trust you can check

Calimero operates the nodes but cannot read your app's data, and it cannot put new code in front of it unless your
cluster's rules give it that decision. Those rules (who approves new node images, who upgrades apps, who approves
outbound hosts) are fixed when the cluster is created, attested with every receipt, and visible to every user. See
the [security model](/security/).
