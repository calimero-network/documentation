An **organization** on a cluster is who can use a set of app instances. It lives in the cluster's own state: the
console never holds it, and the cluster enforces it on every request, on every node.

<OrgDiagram />

## Roles

Every member has one role:

| Role | May |
| --- | --- |
| `guest` | Reach the organization's instances whose access is `org` |
| `member` | Also own instances in the organization |
| `admin` | Manage members and their roles (below owner), capabilities, teams and metadata; delete the organization's instances |
| `owner` | Everything, plus ownership, release approvals and deleting the organization. There is exactly one. |

Beyond their role, members hold **capabilities**, such as creating instances (`CREATE_APP`), inviting people
(`INVITE_MEMBERS`) or upgrading the organization's instances (`MANAGE_APPLICATION`). Admins and team leads hold all of
them where they manage.

## Teams

Teams nest, and each is one of two kinds:

- **Open**: whoever reaches the parent team (at the top, the organization) is a member too.
- **Restricted**: only the people added to it. Nothing flows through a restricted team to the teams below it.

A team's members have a role in it: `lead`, `member` or `read-only`. A read-only member reaches the team's
instances, and a call of theirs that would write anything is refused.

## Who reaches an instance

Every instance carries one access rule, set when it is created and changed by its owner:

| Access | Who reaches the instance |
| --- | --- |
| `org` | Every member of its organization. The default inside an organization. |
| `team` | The team's members. |
| `accounts` | The owner and the listed accounts. |
| `owner` | The owner only. The default outside an organization. |
| `public` | Anyone, signed in or not. |

Someone who does not reach an instance gets `404`: its existence is not disclosed. Finer rules, such as who may
edit which record, are your app's own code, which reads the caller's role and teams with `env::caller_org()`.

## Invites

An admin, or a member with `INVITE_MEMBERS`, creates an invite: a token (`inv_…`) with a role and, optionally, the
teams it adds to. Only its hash is stored. It expires after 7 days and admits one account unless set otherwise. The
person opens it, signs up with a passkey on the cluster and redeems it.

```ts
const token = await client.invite(org, 'member');   // send it to them
await theirClient.redeemInvite(token);               // after they sign up
```

In the console, adding someone to a project invites them to the project's organization the same way.

## Several customers

Two shapes work, chosen per app:

- **One organization and one instance per customer.** Separate state, members and upgrades. Your frontend (or the
  console) creates the organization and the instance with that customer's init arguments.
- **One instance, a restricted team per customer.** One shared state; your app's code keeps customers' records apart
  using the caller's teams.

The full rules, including every capability, are in the core reference's
[Organizations, teams & access](https://calimero-network.github.io/core/protocol/organizations/).
